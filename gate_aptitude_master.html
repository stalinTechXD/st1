Verbal Aptitude

Grammar, vocabulary, analogies, and reading comprehension — ~1/3 of the aptitude marks.

Subject–Verb Agreement

🪝 Analogy: Subject & verb are dance partners — both must be singular or both plural. If one steps small, the other steps small.
  • "Each / every / neither / one of" → singular verb.
  • Ignore words between subject & verb: "The box of chocolates is..."
  • "A number of" → plural; "The number of" → singular.
⚠️ Trap: long phrase inserted to hide the real subject. Bracket it out.

Verbal Analogies high yield

🪝 Analogy: It's a lock & key — find the relationship in pair 1, cut the same key for pair 2.
A : B :: C : ?  →  "A is to B as C is to ?"
Common relations: part→whole, cause→effect, worker→tool, degree (warm→hot), synonym, antonym.
✅ Make a bridge sentence: "A Doctor works in a Hospital" → "A Teacher works in a School."

Vocabulary in Context

🪝 Analogy: A word is a chameleon — its meaning takes the colour of the sentence around it.
  • Use tone clues: positive vs negative charge of the sentence.
  • Prefix/root/suffix cracks unknowns: bene=good, mal=bad, a/an=without.

Reading Comprehension

🪝 Analogy: The passage is a house; questions ask for a specific room. Don't re-read the whole house — go to the room.
  • Read the question first, then scan for keywords.
  • Inference ≠ opinion: answer must be provable from the text.
⚠️ Trap: options that are true in real life but not stated in the passage.
Q. "Oar : Rowing :: Needle : ?"
Show solution
Bridge: an oar is the tool used for rowing. So we need the activity a needle is the tool for.
Answer: Sewing / Stitching.

Number System

Divisibility, factors, HCF/LCM, remainders, units digit — the bedrock.

Divisibility Shortcuts

🪝 Analogy: Each divisor is a fussy doorman checking one feature before letting a number in.
÷ byRule
2last digit even
3digit sum ÷ 3
4last 2 digits ÷ 4
8last 3 digits ÷ 8
9digit sum ÷ 9
11alt-sum of digits ÷ 11

HCF & LCM

🪝 Analogy: HCF = biggest tile that paves two rooms exactly. LCM = soonest two runners meet again at the start line.
HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers
✅ "Bells toll together again" / "tiles" / "min length" → LCM. "Largest tile / max people / exact groups" → HCF.

Units Digit & Cyclicity

🪝 Analogy: Powers cycle like days of the week — the last digit repeats every 4 steps.
Cycle length divides 4 → take power mod 4
2: 2,4,8,6 · 3: 3,9,7,1 · 7: 7,9,3,1 · 8: 8,4,2,6

Remainders

🪝 Analogy: Remainder is the change left in your pocket after buying as many items as possible.
Remainders multiply: rem(a·b) = rem(a)·rem(b) mod n
✅ Big remainders → replace each number by its own remainder first, then combine.
Q. Find the units digit of 7¹⁵³.
Show solution
7's cycle = 7,9,3,1 (length 4). 153 mod 4 = 1 → take the 1st in cycle.
Answer: 7.

Percentages, Profit & Interest

The single most reused idea across all of aptitude.

Percentage Core

🪝 Analogy: "Per-cent" = out of 100. Imagine everything scaled to a ₹100 pizza — 20% is simply 20 slices.
x% of N = (x/100)·N  |  change% = (new−old)/old ×100
⚠️ Trap: +20% then −20% ≠ 0. It's a net −4% (multiply 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96). Successive % never add.

Profit & Loss

🪝 Analogy: Cost Price is what you paid the shop; Selling Price is what the next buyer paid you. Profit% always rides on CP.
Profit% = (SP−CP)/CP ×100  |  SP = CP·(1 + P%/100)
⚠️ Trap: discount % is on marked price, profit % is on cost price — different bases!

Simple vs Compound Interest

🪝 Analogy: SI = a flat salary every year. CI = a snowball — interest earns its own interest.
SI = P·R·T/100  |  CI: A = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ
✅ For 2 years: CI − SI = P·(R/100)². Instant one-liner.

Fraction ↔ % cheat table

🪝 Analogy: These are your speed-dial numbers — memorise once, save 20 seconds each.
1/21/31/41/51/8
50%33.3%25%20%12.5%
Q. A price rises 25% then falls 20%. Net change?
Show solution
Multiply factors: 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00.
Answer: No net change (0%). The rise and fall cancel exactly here.

Ratio & Proportion

The language of "for every…".

Ratio Basics

🪝 Analogy: A ratio is a recipe: 2:3 flour to sugar. Double the cake → keep the recipe, scale both by k.
a:b = ka:kb  |  if a:b and b:c → a:b:c via common b
✅ Introduce a multiplier x: ages 3:4 become 3x and 4x, then use the given condition to find x.

Proportion & Variation

🪝 Analogy: Direct = see-saw kids of equal weight (up together). Inverse = speed vs time (faster → less time).
Direct: y = kx  |  Inverse: xy = k
⚠️ Trap: "more workers, less time" is inverse — don't blindly cross-multiply as direct.
Q. Ages of A:B = 3:4, sum = 42. Find B's age.
Show solution
Let ages = 3x, 4x. Sum 7x = 42 → x = 6. B = 4x.
Answer: 24.

Averages, Mixtures & Alligation

Balancing values around a centre.

Average

🪝 Analogy: Average is a see-saw pivot — the balance point where highs and lows cancel.
Average = Sum / Count  |  Sum = Average × Count
✅ New person changes average by Δ across (n+1) people → their value = old avg + (n+1)Δ.

Alligation (Mixtures)

🪝 Analogy: Cheap tea & costly tea meet at a tug-of-war; the ratio of quantities is the inverse of their distance from the mean price.
Cheap : Dear = (Dear − Mean) : (Mean − Cheap)
⚠️ Trap: the ratio comes out swapped — the quantity nearer the mean is larger.
Q. Mix tea at ₹20 and ₹30 to get ₹24/kg. Ratio?
Show solution
Distances: |30−24|=6 and |24−20|=4. Ratio 20-tea : 30-tea = 6 : 4.
Answer: 3 : 2.

Time, Speed & Distance

Trains, boats, and relative motion.

The Master Triangle

🪝 Analogy: Cover the quantity you want with your thumb — the other two show the formula. D on top, S and T below.
D = S × T  |  S = D/T  |  T = D/S
✅ km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18. m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5.

Relative Speed

🪝 Analogy: Same direction = two runners → gap shrinks by the difference. Opposite = sum (they rush at each other).
Same dir: |S₁−S₂|  |  Opposite: S₁+S₂
⚠️ Trap: a train passing a pole covers its own length; passing a platform covers length + platform.

Boats & Streams

🪝 Analogy: The river is a moving walkway. Downstream = walkway helps; upstream = walkway fights you.
Down = b + s  |  Up = b − s  |  b = (Down+Up)/2

Average Speed

🪝 Analogy: Equal distances → NOT the plain average. Use the harmonic mean (time weights the slow leg more).
Equal distance: Avg = 2S₁S₂/(S₁+S₂)
⚠️ Trap: never just do (S₁+S₂)/2 for a round trip.
Q. Go 60 km/h, return 40 km/h, same road. Average speed?
Show solution
Equal distance → harmonic mean: 2·60·40/(60+40) = 4800/100.
Answer: 48 km/h (not 50!).

Time & Work

Pipes, workers, and shared jobs.

Rate Thinking

🪝 Analogy: Think in work-per-day, not days. If A finishes in 10 days, A does 1/10 of the job each day — a steady dripping tap.
Together: 1/T = 1/a + 1/b  →  T = ab/(a+b)
✅ LCM trick: let total work = LCM of the days. Then each person's rate is a clean integer.

Pipes & Cisterns

🪝 Analogy: Filling pipe = income (+), leak/outlet = expense (−). Net rate is your savings per day.
Net rate = Σ(inlets) − Σ(outlets)
⚠️ Trap: an outlet's rate is subtracted. Sign errors kill this topic.
Q. A does a job in 12 days, B in 6 days. Together?
Show solution
Let work = LCM(12,6)=12 units. A=1/day, B=2/day → together 3/day. 12 ÷ 3.
Answer: 4 days.

Permutations, Combinations & Probability

Counting without listing, and measuring chance.

Permutation vs Combination

🪝 Analogy: Permutation = a race podium (order matters: gold≠silver). Combination = a fruit basket (order irrelevant).
nPr = n!/(n−r)!  |  nCr = n!/[r!(n−r)!]
✅ Keyword scan: "arrange / rank / seat / password" → P. "select / choose / team / handshake" → C.

Probability

🪝 Analogy: Probability = (lucky doors) / (all doors). A fair coin has 1 lucky door out of 2.
P = favourable / total  |  P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)
✅ "At least one" → compute 1 − P(none). Almost always faster.
⚠️ Trap: "and" (both) → multiply; "or" (either) → add (then subtract overlap).
Q. In how many ways can 3 of 5 people stand in a row?
Show solution
Order matters (a row) → permutation. 5P3 = 5·4·3.
Answer: 60.

Progressions & Number Series

Spotting the hidden pattern.

AP & GP

🪝 Analogy: AP = climbing equal stairs (add d). GP = a rumour spreading (multiply by r each round).
AP: Tₙ=a+(n−1)d, Sₙ=n/2·(2a+(n−1)d)
GP: Tₙ=a·r^(n−1), Sₙ=a(rⁿ−1)/(r−1)

Cracking a Series

🪝 Analogy: Be a detective — check the "gaps" between terms first; the difference often confesses the rule.
  • Constant gap → AP.
  • Constant ratio → GP.
  • Gaps form their own pattern → 2nd-level difference.
  • Watch for squares, cubes, primes, ±alternating.
Q. 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
Show solution
Gaps: 4,6,8,10 → next gap 12. Also terms = n(n+1): 1·2, 2·3, 3·4… next = 6·7.
Answer: 42.

Data Interpretation

Reading tables, bar/pie/line charts fast and accurately.

Reading Charts

🪝 Analogy: A chart is a map. Read the legend + axis units first, like checking a map's scale before walking.
  • Pie: whole = 360° = 100%. 1% = 3.6°.
  • Compare via ratios/percentages, not raw eyeballing.
  • Approximate — GATE options are usually well-spaced.
⚠️ Trap: mixing up "percentage change" with "percentage point change".

Speed Tactics

✅ Do the reading (units, totals) once; reuse for all sub-questions.
✅ Round smartly: 48.7% ≈ 50% to eliminate options, then refine only if needed.
🪝 Analogy: Treat the dataset like a buffet — grab only the numbers the question orders, ignore the rest.

Logical & Analytical Reasoning

Deduction, syllogisms, seating, blood relations, statements.

Syllogisms

🪝 Analogy: Draw Venn circles — reasoning becomes seeing which balloons overlap.
  • "All A are B" → A-circle sits inside B.
  • "Some A are B" → circles overlap.
  • A conclusion holds only if it's true in every valid diagram.
⚠️ Trap: "possibility" conclusions — if any diagram allows it, it can be true.

Blood Relations

🪝 Analogy: Build a family tree on paper: + for male, − for female, horizontal = siblings/spouse, vertical = parent/child.
✅ "My father's only son" = myself. Read pronouns of the speaker carefully.

Seating / Arrangement

🪝 Analogy: You're a wedding planner — place the most-constrained guest first, then fit the rest around them.
  • Circle facing centre: left/right flip vs a row.
  • Start with fixed/definite clues, keep possibilities in a grid.

Statement & Assumption / Cube

🪝 Analogy: An assumption is the hidden floor a statement stands on — remove it and the statement collapses.
✅ Cube cut into n³ small cubes: corners=8 (3 faces), edges=12(n−2), faces=6(n−2)², inside=(n−2)³.
Q. A 3×3×3 cube is painted then cut into 27 unit cubes. How many have exactly 2 painted faces?
Show solution
Exactly-2-faces = edge cubes = 12·(n−2) with n=3 → 12·1.
Answer: 12.

Spatial Aptitude

Shape rotation, mirrors, paper folding, embedded figures.

Rotation & Mirrors

🪝 Analogy: Rotation = spinning a photo (shape unchanged). Mirror = your reflection (left↔right swapped, R becomes Я).
✅ Track one unique landmark (an arrow/dot) through the transform instead of the whole figure.
⚠️ Trap: confusing rotation with reflection — a rotated figure keeps its handedness, a mirrored one flips it.

Paper Folding & Cubes

🪝 Analogy: Punched holes are symmetric twins — each unfold doubles them across the crease line.
  • Dice: opposite faces of a standard die sum to 7.
  • Net folding: opposite faces are never adjacent in the net.
